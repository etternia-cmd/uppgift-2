Electronics

Another passion of mine is electronics. It started at a very early age when I got my first laptop from my mother and later on when I got my first gaming console, being the PSP 6000. The first thing that made me curious about electronics was when I wanted to play this game called "The Evil Within", upon trying to install it I couldnt run it because my moms laptop was a 2009 ASUS laptop with 1GB of DDR2 ram, no GPU and a single core pentium processor. Upon looking as for why the game couldnt run on my laptop thats when I realized that every technology was compriced of different parts, which made the computer run. The better the parts the fast the computer and so forth. After that I researched more into it, turns out even though my PSP was also technically a computer, it had completely different parts. Further more, the parts on the PSP were optimized to run specifically for the PSP itself so I couldnt for example take parts from it and put them on my laptop.

Later on I talked to my uncle as he is an electrical engineer that helped build the Bulgarian Metro System (one of the most used and beloved forms of transportation in our country) as well as having worked on other projects as well. He explained to me that different electronics function wildly different. He showed me a broken toaster at the time which he was trying to salvage/repair (something he has been doing ever since he was little) and even though the toaster and a PC follow the same fundamental electronic concepts, their complexity is on a completely different level. A regular toaster would basically have a power input, some sort of heating element depending on the toaster, a control mechanism (something that regulates how long the bread is gonna toast) temperature sensors and what not. A very basic toaster wouldnt necesserily require a CPU or RAM unlike a PC or a gaming console (although nowadays the more advanced toasters might incorporate them such as toasters that have screens and such). The basic idea is that that you have a lever that closes a switch which in turn makes the heating element receive power, it converts the power from electrical energy to thermal energy which is regulated by some sort of a timer and a thermostat. That timer and thermostat determines when to stop the toaster in which case the lever opens the switch which kills the power and makes the bread pop up. Some modern toasters could use some sort of a microcontroller where the timer and temperature sensor is controlled but those microcomputers dont require nearly as much CPU power nor RAM as much a regular PC that requires up to 4GB of RAM just to run windows 11. And the toaster isn't the only example, technology and electronics are literally everywhere around us today, lights in the house, our stoves, TV's, phones everything and the software in it is what's programmed in order to run the electronics in the way we want.

To this day I like looking at different deals that I could find for computers specifically, the first computer I ever built (or more like helped building) was my brothers PC when I was around 10 years old. It had a GT 730 GPU a staggering 8GBs of DDR3 RAM and an intel i5 4th gen processor along with a power supply, case and motherboard which was salvaged from an old shop that didn't need their computer. I've build the computer that im using today and writing this, im also trying to find a good deal for a better GPU and an SSD but that is proving to be rather difficult given today's market prices.
